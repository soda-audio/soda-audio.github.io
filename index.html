Scaling Open Discrete Audio Foundation Models with Interleaved Semantic, Acoustic, and Text Tokens

1Stanford University, 2SCB 10X, 3University of Southern California, 4University of Cambridge, 5OpenAthena

Abstract

Current audio language models are predominantly text-first, either extending pre-trained text LLM backbones or relying on semantic-only audio tokens, limiting general audio modeling. This paper presents a systematic empirical study of native audio foundation models that apply next-token prediction to audio at scale, jointly modeling semantic content, acoustic details, and text to support both general audio generation and cross-modal capabilities.

We provide comprehensive empirical insights for building such models: (1) We systematically investigate design choices—data sources, text mixture ratios, and token composition—establishing a validated training recipe. (2) We conduct the first scaling law study for discrete audio models via IsoFLOP analysis on 64 models spanning 3×1018 to 3×1020 FLOPs, finding that optimal data grows 1.6× faster than optimal model size. (3) We apply these lessons to train SODA (Scaling Open Discrete Audio), a suite of models from 135M to 4B parameters on 500B tokens, comparing against our scaling predictions and existing models.

SODA serves as a flexible backbone for diverse audio/text tasks—we demonstrate this by fine-tuning for voice-preserving speech-to-speech translation, using the same unified architecture.

Token Composition Strategies

We explore three token composition strategies for training discrete audio models. Our utterance-level interleaved approach combines semantic, acoustic, and text tokens to enable cross-modal capabilities.

Scaling Laws for Discrete Audio

We conduct the first scaling law study for discrete audio models via IsoFLOP analysis on 64 models spanning 3×1018 to 3×1020 FLOPs. Our key finding: optimal data scales faster than optimal model size.

The fitted scaling laws reveal: N* ∝ C0.367 and D* ∝ C0.579, where data scales 1.6× faster than model size. This differs from Chinchilla's equal scaling and suggests discrete audio tokens carry lower information density than text.

Validation Loss as a Reliable Metric

Before conducting IsoFLOP analysis, we validated that validation loss (NLL) on held-out audio data strongly correlates with downstream task performance across all 64 models.

SODA: Scaling Open Discrete Audio

Applying insights from our scaling laws and design choices, we train SODA, a suite of models from 135M to 4B parameters on 500B tokens (up to 1.3×1022 FLOPs).

We compare cold-start (random initialization) versus warm-start (from text LLMs) training at scale, finding that cold-start is superior and provides higher training stability.

SODA achieves competitive performance across audio and cross-modal benchmarks. We further validate SODA as a flexible backbone by fine-tuning for voice-preserving speech-to-speech translation, simply formulated as a next-token prediction task.
